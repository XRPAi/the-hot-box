# Triple 14 — Friday, September 11, 2026

**TL;DR** — the day in port + freight.

No verified same-day dwell, chassis-pool, or berth-queue figures cleared verification for any of the four gateways this cycle, so this brief is filed as a watch-list rather than a numbers report. The one live disruption with a named operational consequence is the Panama Canal: as of an August 20 report, more than 100 ships were queued for transit and the Panama Canal Authority put the average wait for non-booked vessels at eight days, driven by low water forcing draft restrictions and by Strait of Hormuz disruption pushing tankers onto alternate routes [1]. Port of Los Angeles executive director Gene Seroka said on August 18 that the situation could divert an additional 5 percent of volume to LA, with contingency planning underway with the Harbor Trucking Association, marine terminal operators, container lines and dockworkers [1]. Treat West Coast capacity as the exposure to price this month; treat Savannah and Houston as unverified until the port advisories are pulled directly.

**Port snapshot** — dwell, backups, chassis at LA/LB, NY/NJ, Savannah, Houston.

**LA/LB.** No current dwell or anchorage count is verifiable today. For calibration, healthy import dwell runs 2–3 days and 6-plus days signals congestion, roughly 30 percent of LA boxes move inland by rail, and vessel queues above about 20 ships have historically preceded dwell spikes of 40–60 percent within seven to ten days [2]. The most recent authoritative move-type breakout in the fetched record is September 2024, not current conditions: complex rail dwell hit 9.25 days against 8.2 days in August while truck dwell ran 3.21 days, with LBCT holding rail dwell near four days and Long Beach's six terminals averaging seven [3]. Crisis-scale reference: 102 containerships in San Pedro Bay, 73 at anchor, in October 2021 [4].

**NY/NJ.** The only terminal-level operational detail confirmed is Port Newark Container Terminal's posted notice that the PNCT reefer gate is extended to 4:30 p.m. this week [5] — relevant if you are routing perishables through Newark today. No berth-queue or average-waiting-time figure for the harbor is available from the fetched sources.

**Savannah and Houston.** No material development in the fetched sources this cycle — no dwell, gate, chassis, or berth data for either port.

**Chassis.** Pool utilization is not a public number at any of these gateways; it exceeds 90 percent in peak season and shows up as street dwell, so it has to be inferred from gate turn times and dray rate movement rather than read off a dashboard [2]. The mechanism to watch is documented and self-reinforcing: as turn time worsens, containers are not picked up, stack occupancy rises, and turn times degrade further [6].

**Service advisories** — weather, labor, rail connections.

No NHC or NOAA tropical product for the Gulf or South Atlantic cleared verification for this cycle, and no 2026-dated ILA or ILWU advisory is in the fetched record — neither absence should be read as an all-clear during peak hurricane season, and both should be checked at source before you commit a Gulf or South Atlantic booking today. On rail, PANYNJ states the port operates on-dock and near-dock ExpressRail service with CSX and Norfolk Southern and holds the largest rail lift capacity on the U.S. East Coast [7], which is the relevant relief valve if East Coast import volume rises on Panama diversions.

**Regulatory** — port authority rulings, demurrage rules, tariffs.

Diarize Newark. PNCT lists the current NYTC tariff as effective 10-01-2025, with a successor tariff already posted effective 10-05-2026 [5]; dockage, demurrage and detention exposure at Newark should be repriced against the successor document before that date, not after. On the West Coast, the standing precedent for dwell penalties is the LA/LB Container Dwell Fee announced October 25, 2021: $100 per container, escalating $100 per day, triggered at nine days for truck-bound boxes and three days for rail-bound boxes, covering roughly 40 percent of on-terminal containers at the time [4]. The fetched sources do not establish its current status — do not assume it is live, and confirm with the ports before quoting it to a customer.

**Signals worth watching** — what to track next.

Three things move the week. First, whether the Panama queue and the eight-day non-booked wait hold or ease [1] — that determines whether Seroka's 5 percent diversion scenario becomes real West Coast volume [1]. Second, the San Pedro Bay vessel count against the 20-ship threshold, which is the earliest reliable warning of a dwell spike seven to ten days out [2]. Third, dray turn times and spot dray rates as the only usable proxy for chassis tightness [2]. For filing a numbers-complete brief, pull POLA/POLB signal dashboards, PMSA monthly dwell, Georgia Ports Authority and Port Houston terminal advisories, IMF PortWatch daily queue counts, and NHC products directly — the gaps in today's snapshot are source gaps, not quiet conditions.
